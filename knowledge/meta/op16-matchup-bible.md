## 3.1 UG Luffy

**Difficulty: Easy — Preferred Start: First**

### Overview

For the UG Luffy matchup, I think this is one of the easiest ones to play in the format. I think it is a favored matchup, and honestly, sometimes it feels almost like a mirror match.

The key to this matchup is knowing how to manage your resources properly, since UG Luffy is going to be one of the best decks in the meta at creating value. One of the most important things is to not take too much damage randomly, but also to understand that Luffy is one of those decks that will have symmetrical swings throughout the game. This means that even if you counter a small swing in the early game, you will probably receive much bigger swings in the late game, and at that point you might be forced to take them because your resources will be much lower.

I think one of the keys to winning this matchup is understanding which characters you need to remove immediately and which ones you can afford to leave on the board for a bit before getting rid of them. You also need to know which characters you should develop yourself, and how you should swing into their board most of the time.

One of the biggest mistakes I have seen from Enel players, which I also faced myself while playing UG Luffy, is that they were focusing too much on developing 6c Enel, while basically neglecting the raw power of cards like Reiju. They were also wasting resources like El Thor on characters that had already cycled themselves, which made them lose a lot of tempo.

I think the general game plan is to try to push them as much as possible, develop multiple Reiju, and eventually play 6c Enel on curve if you have the time. You should use El Thor only in spots where it actually gives you a big advantage, or where it basically shuts down your opponent's whole board.

Removing the 6k bodies whenever they hit the board is also one of the keys to winning the game, and always having a 1-cost character to swing with is one of the best things you can have on the board.

**Mulligan cheatsheet** (`media/mulligan-ug-luffy.png`) : Ohm, Shura, Charlotte Pudding, Satori, Vinsmoke Reiju, Mamaragan.

### Early game

For the early game, I think our main focus should be developing as many 1-cost characters as possible, while also developing our Reiju.

This is one of those matchups where you want to develop your 6c Enel, but you have a limited window to do so. Most of the time, if you can afford to develop it within the first two turns after you reach the 6 DON!! mark, I think it is very good. However, if you need to develop 6c Enel in the late game, it can become a problem.

That is because most UG Luffy players will start freezing it, and you will basically give up an entire turn just to develop 6c Enel. In the late game, they will only need around 2 DON!! to freeze it forever by playing Mr. 3 together with other cards.

One of the best cards in this matchup is, of course, Reiju, because she can both put pressure in some spots and be very defensive in the late game. As long as you see multiple Reiju, the matchup becomes much easier. The same applies to Ohm, which allows you to save a lot of events like Lightning Beast Kiten, because you will be able to minus most of the 6k bodies your opponent develops.

The key to the early game is to gather as many resources as possible, fill your board with Reiju and 1-cost characters, and eventually develop 6c Enel if you are able to do so.

The threats your opponent develops need to be managed as soon as they hit the board, especially Bon Clay, Mr. 1, 6k Crocodile, Buggy, and Prisoner. These are cards you usually want to remove on the same turn they are played. If you are not able to remove two of them at the same time, be prepared to remove the next threat they play together with the one they will eventually rest.

The Freeze event can also be really good in this phase of the game, because if you are not able to remove one of the 6k bodies, you can always freeze it and remove it later.

Try to remember that they also have a lot of 2k counters, so going for swings like 5k and 7k is usually better than going 6k-6k. Especially if you have Ohm on the board, swinging 6k into 4k is usually one of the best attacks you can make.

You should also try to pressure their life during this stage of the game, especially if you can manage to put them at low life.

### Mid game

When it comes to the mid game, usually the best thing you can do is try to understand who is imposing the race on whom.

Most of the time, the matchup against UG Luffy is a race for damage, and if the UG Luffy player is good, they will try to trim your resources as much as possible by making swings that force you to commit cards from your hand. Most of the time, they will swing for 6k, so you need to be careful not to break your hand with counters. In many spots, you will also need your 2k counters, which are basically your events, in hand if you want to stop some of their aggression.

Life management is important here, but most of the time you can also drop down to 2 or 3 life, because many of their swings are going to be symmetrical. For this reason, you need to use your El Thor wisely.

What I recommend is to use El Thor only when it is actually meaningful. If you think about it, using El Thor on a small character is not always correct, because if you remove it before they even attack, they basically swung for 6k without even needing to assign DON!! since you invested El Thor to trade an already cycled card. So try to manage your El Thor properly. Don't just shoot them blindly in the early game. Instead, wait for the mid game to understand which characters you can remove, whether you can eventually clear the whole board without committing too many swings into their characters, and at the same time how much you need to pressure your opponent.

One of the best ways to beat UG Luffy players is to put pressure on them with life-lead swings, so 7k and 9k swings are usually premium. Avoiding 6k swings is also smart, since they have access to a lot of 2k counters.

Having said that, your objective for the mid game should be to establish at least two meaningful bodies, ideally at least one Reiju and one 6c Enel. This will usually force your opponent into freezing one of them, while you can keep pushing damage with the other. At the same time, you should use the rest of your board, like your Leader and your 1-cost characters, to trade into their board.

### Late game

When it comes to the late game, I think your main focus should be trying to close out the game before they do.

You don't need to pay too much attention to cards like the restand event, because it will be very difficult for them to stick a full board with different names. However, I would be careful about always having an answer for an eventual Boa Hancock.

What I have noticed is that games in this matchup often drag for a very long time. Then, eventually, in the late game, if the UG Luffy player is very good, they will just drop a Boa Hancock after you have already used most of your resources, forcing you to use bad removals to deal with it. For this reason, I would always try to keep at least one Senior Pink or Gamma Knife in my hand for that situation, and I would not waste cards like Mamaragan too much, because you want to keep at least one way to remove that threat.

Having said that, you should try to develop as many Blockers as possible without exposing them to free trades, while also keeping up with the push you need in order to kill them.

Just remember that they can generate a lot of hand value, so it is really important to maintain pressure and try to hit them with as many swings as possible, whether they are 5k, 7k, 9k, or higher. This is very important because if you drag the game for too long, they will eventually win, since they can generate more value than you.

### General tips

- UG Luffy is a favored matchup, but it requires very good resource management because both decks can generate a lot of value throughout the game.
- In the early game, focus on developing 1-cost characters, Reiju, and eventually 6c Enel within the right window, before it becomes too easy for them to freeze it with Mr. 3.
- Remove their main 6k bodies as soon as possible, especially Bon Clay, Mr. 1, Crocodile, Buggy, and Prisoner, because letting them stay on board makes their pressure much harder to manage.
- Use El Thor only when it gives you real value. Do not waste it blindly on small characters that already cycled themselves, unless it helps you clear the board or protect your tempo.
- In the mid game, understand who is leading the damage race. Try to pressure them with 7k and 9k swings, while avoiding too many 6k swings because of their high number of 2k counters.
- In the late game, keep an answer ready for Boa Hancock, usually Senior Pink, Gamma Knife, or Mamaragan, while developing Blockers and closing the game before UG Luffy outvalues you.

## 3.2 BY Teach

**Difficulty: Hard — Preferred Start: First**

**Leader BY Teach (OP16-080, 5000, 4 Life)** — `media/leader-by-teach-op16-080.webp` :
- *Opponent's Turn* : all of your Characters gain +1 cost.
- *On Your Opponent's Attack, Once Per Turn* : you may trash 1 card with a Trigger from your hand → change the target of that attack to this Leader or to one of your {Blackbeard Pirates} type Characters (l'effet de « redirection » qui rend nos trades difficiles).

### Overview

The overview for the BY Teach matchup is that it is one of the most difficult matchups in the current format. This is mainly because Teach can drag the game for a very long time and eventually shut us down in the late game with powerful characters that we cannot easily deal with, such as 10c Teach, which basically denies our 6c Enel, and cards like 4c Burgess, which are very difficult to remove.

In addition to that, it will be very difficult for us to trade characters most of the time, even though we have access to Gamma Knife, because they will always have the redirection effect from their Leader. Eventually, it also becomes very difficult to aggro them.

For this reason, the best strategy against BY Teach is usually to clear the board as they develop it. Having access to as many Gamma Knife as possible from the early game to the late game is one of the key parts of winning this matchup.

Of course, developing multiple 6c Enel in the early game is also very important, since they allow us to apply the pressure we need in order to finish the game before BY Teach reaches the late-game stage, where it becomes much more difficult to kill them.

Generally speaking, this matchup is also one of the reasons why we wanted to increase the number of removals in our current build. We will see if the deck becomes very popular in the upcoming tournaments, but for the time being, it is played by a very small percentage of players. Still, it remains one of the most difficult matchups, since the Leader basically counters Enel.

The strategy is very simple: you want to go wide with your characters, and you want to remove all the threats they develop, especially the first Burgess and Shiryu, and eventually also the 8c Teach that they will start playing.

Then, once they reach the 10 DON!! mark and start playing cards like 10c Teach, we will need cards such as Divine Departure, which we decided to run in two copies, to deal with those threats.

At the same time, we don't really care about pushing a lot of damage. We mainly want to trim resources from them, so going for many swings instead of tall ones is usually better.

**Mulligan cheatsheet** (`media/mulligan-by-teach.png`) : Ohm, Shura, Charlotte Pudding, Satori, Vinsmoke Reiju, Mamaragan. Le texte insiste : trouver **Gamma Knife** est la priorité de ce matchup.

### Early game

The early game against BY Teach is very straightforward. You need to focus on removing your opponent's threats, mainly 4c Burgess, which is one of the most important cards we want to remove, together with 6c Shiryu.

This is why, in the mulligan, it is so important to find Gamma Knife, because it allows you to perform removals on these early threats.

When it comes to characters, you don't mind developing 4-cost bodies together with 6c Enel, but you need to keep in mind that you always want to go wide with your 1-cost characters, especially cards like Ohm, which can really help you remove threats in the early game.

Be careful, because BY Teach players will starve you most of the time, so using your resources properly is very important. This is also why Senior Pink is crucial in this matchup, since it helps you find the missing piece of the puzzle to remove the early threats.

If you manage to navigate this early phase by removing cards like 4c Burgess as soon as they hit the board, while also developing some threats of your own, the mid game will look much easier for you.

When it comes to swings, you don't really care about how big they are, as long as you are making multiple attacks. Eventually, your focus is just to remove cards from your opponent's hand and life, while your main objective is to generate as many resources as possible.

### Mid game

For the mid game, the gameplay is basically the same. You need to keep focusing on removing their threats.

Just remember that against cards like Shiryu, you will also be able to remove them with El Thor, while 4c Burgess can usually only be removed with Mamaragan together with Gamma Knife. If your opponent rests their pieces, it will become much easier for us to remove them.

When 8c Teach hits the board, you need to be careful, because that card is going to be one of the most problematic threats they can develop. Even if we use Gamma Knife on that body, it will still be a 5k character, and removing it is not going to be easy unless you developed a strong board in the early game that allows you to put enough swings into it.

The mid game is very similar to the early game. You still want to manage your resources, cycle away events you don't need, such as the Freeze event, and eventually find the missing pieces to remove your opponent's board.

Just keep in mind that your main objective has to be developing meaningful bodies while removing your opponent's board at the same time. If BY Teach does not have a board, they will not be able to gain value when they start delaying your turns.

So keep focusing on finding the missing resources, and don't waste your removals on cards you don't need to remove. For example, don't use El Thor on small characters unless it enables you to remove a bigger threat, and do not waste Mamaragan either.

Since your resources will be limited, especially in this phase of the game, it is very important to use them only on threats that are big enough to justify your removals.

### Late game

For the late game, I think nothing changes too much, except for the fact that we will also need to focus on removing 10c Teach, unless we are already very advanced with our aggression.

In this phase, you can make use of Divine Departure to remove 10c Teach, and having cards like Ohm, or adding combinations like Lightning Beast Kiten plus El Thor, is usually one of the best ways to deal with that threat.

Having said that, it is really important to reach this phase with BY Teach not having a decent board. If they have access to those cards while already having characters in play, they will start snowballing, and they can basically kill us in a matter of three turns.

This is why it is really important to play both the early game and the mid game very well, because that is the key to winning this matchup. If you do not manage to remove their board in the early to mid game, it will be very difficult to win the late game.

One of the other possible ways to win, if you miss the early removals, is to develop 6c Enel back-to-back. Usually, having multiple 6c Enel on the board can allow you to win the game even without removals, but I still think this line is too risky and also very difficult to achieve.

For this reason, you should keep focusing on removing their board. Then, once you reach the super late game, you should stop focusing too much on board removal and try to push to win the game as fast as possible.

Just remember that BY Teach has access to plays like double Borsalino, and a double Borsalino turn that heals 2 life, puts two Blockers on the board, and combines with the Leader effect can easily become a game-winning play. Keep that in mind, and try to reach that part of the game with a good board already developed.

### General tips

- BY Teach is one of the hardest matchups in the format because the Leader effect makes trading difficult, while cards like 10c Teach, 8c Teach, 4c Burgess, and 6c Shiryu can easily take over the game.
- Your main objective is to clear their board as they develop it. If BY Teach reaches the late game with an established board, it becomes extremely difficult to win.
- Gamma Knife is one of the most important cards in the matchup, especially in the early and mid game, because it allows you to deal with threats like 4c Burgess, 6c Shiryu, and eventually helps set up removals on bigger bodies.
- Senior Pink is crucial because it helps you find the missing pieces for your removal turns, while cards like Ohm, Lightning Beast Kiten, El Thor, and Mamaragan should be used carefully to remove only meaningful threats.
- Developing multiple 6c Enel early can be one of your best ways to apply pressure and force BY Teach to react, but relying only on this plan is risky if you cannot also remove their board.
- In the late game, you need to be ready for 10c Teach with cards like Divine Departure, while also respecting strong defensive turns like double Borsalino plus Leader effect. Once you reach the super late game, stop over-focusing on board removal and try to close the game as fast as possible.

## 3.3 PY Rosinante

**Difficulty: Easy — Preferred Start: Second**

> ⚠️ Note du coach local : la data sim (`matchups.md`) donne 70,1 % en commençant vs 62,1 % en 2e — le « Preferred Start: Second » du guide est un choix de ligne experte (deny des effets Law), pas ce que dit la data brute. Point à clarifier avec le pro.

### Overview

The PY Rosinante matchup is one of the trickiest ones, since we need to manage our DON!! properly in order to deny the effects of their best characters.

Of course, it is crucial to understand whether we are going first or second, because our game plan changes slightly depending on that. One of the keys to this matchup is understanding what the Rosinante deck actually wants to do.

One of the main takeaways in this matchup is that they always want to play their Law cards on curve, especially the 4c Law, which is a 6k body, and the 6c Rosilaw, which is one of the key cards of their deck. You also need to understand that at any point in the game, they have access to 7c Sakazuki, which can basically remove our 6c Enel.

PY Rosinante is one of the very few decks in the current meta that can actually remove 6c Enel, so we need to be careful about when we swing with it.

Overall, what is important to keep in mind is that if you play the early game very well, and then manage to use Gamma Knife properly on meaningful threats, this matchup should look relatively easy.

Just keep in mind that you still want to play your normal Enel game plan, but you also need to understand that one single removal effect will not always be enough to deal with a threat, since they can protect one Law character with their Leader effect. For this reason, it is really important to always have a double way to deal with any threat you want to commit Gamma Knife on, while also trying to bait them into using their Leader effect before removing those threats.

Keep in mind that PY Rosinante also tends to starve you, so it is really important to manage your resources properly and use your removals and Senior Pink on the correct targets.

**Mulligan cheatsheet** (`media/mulligan-py-rosinante.png`) : Ohm, Shura, Charlotte Pudding, Satori, **6c Enel**.

### Early game — le cœur du matchup : le DON!! denial

The early game really depends on whether you go first or second.

**Going second is preferred in this matchup**, because you want them to be on the going-first curve. This allows us to deny their Leader effect for several turns.

If you think about it, what Rosinante players usually want to do is, on 3 DON!!, minus 1 DON!! to use their Leader effect and then play 4c Law. However, if you are playing Enel and you start second, when you get to your 2 DON!! turn, you can simply play a 1-cost character, **any 1-cost is fine except for Pudding**, and pass with 1 DON!!. This means they will not be able to use 4c Law to ramp, which puts them in a very weird spot.

If you play your first turn by going down to 1 DON!!, they will start their second turn with 3 DON!!, and they will not be able to use their Leader effect and play 4c Law, because then they would not be able to use the effect again on the following turn to play 6c Rosilaw. The Law they play for 2 DON!!, even if it is a 6k body, will not be able to ramp because they will have more DON!! than us.

Most of the time, they will probably pass on 3 DON!!, and the same idea applies on the following turn, where you can basically end your turn on 3 DON!!. Even though this can feel counterintuitive for most Enel players, it is very strong in this matchup.

If you play your second turn using your DON!! and end on 3 DON!!, when they untap, they will have 5 DON!!. Then, by de-ramping 1 DON!! with their Leader effect, even if they want to play 6c Rosilaw, they will still have more DON!! than you. This means they will not be able to ramp or draw a card from 6c Rosilaw.

**When going first** : being at 1 DON!! after your first turn does not really matter. What is really important is that when you pass your second turn, you do not want to end on 4 DON!!, because they will be able to simply play 4c Law raw, ramp up to 5 DON!!, and then start the following turn with access to 7c Sakazuki, which can already threaten our 6c Enel.

Always think about ending your turn with a number of DON!! that denies their Law effects, so they cannot ramp and speed up their game plan, while also denying additional effects like the one from 6c Rosilaw, which would allow them to draw cards.

Your early game focus should be developing your board as usual, and eventually developing 6c Enel on curve. From that point, we will need to protect it and use it to help us remove their threats.

### Mid game

The mid game is very different from the early game. At this point, we do not really care as much about DON!! management, because our opponent will be at a higher number of DON!!, and it will no longer be as important to stay below the 4 DON!! mark.

What becomes important here is understanding when they will be able to remove our 6c Enel. Eventually, playing 7c Sakazuki plus Mamaragan is one way for them to deal with 6c Enel. The best way to deal with that is usually to expose our 6c Enel only if we have access to cards like Reiju on board, which can protect 6c Enel while it is rested.

Most of the time, they will not have that many swings available if they want to play 7c Sakazuki, so it is really important to understand when we can afford to swing with 6c Enel. Eventually, we will need to put pressure on them, but our main focus in this phase should mostly be removing their biggest threats.

Usually, we want to remove cards like 7c Sakazuki and 6c Rosilaw in this order, since they are both 8k bodies. We can easily remove them with Gamma Knife, but be careful because 6c Rosilaw can always be protected once by their Leader effect. This means you usually need two hits to remove it, such as a 5k into 3k swing plus El Thor or two swings.

For this reason, it is really important to commit to a Gamma Knife turn only when we are sure we can actually remove the target. When it comes to 7c Sakazuki, they cannot protect it with the Leader effect, so any combination of removals will be fine. **7c Sakazuki should usually be our main removal target**, because it gives them back a lot of DON!!, which we do not want them to get.

Aside from that, in this phase, we still want to keep developing our board, and most of the time our main objective is to trim resources from them.

Just keep in mind that if they have access to the 1-cost Stage, your DON!! management needs to be a little bit different, especially because they can remove multiple DON!! and then go back to the same number of DON!! as us to play 6c Rosilaw.

It is important to deny as many resources as possible. Also, keep in mind that they play the I Love You event, so swinging for 6k is usually not ideal. Whenever you swing into a character, you want to swing to kill, not just to get resources from their hand.

### Late game

The late game will be basically the same as the mid game. At this point, we need to focus more on starting to accelerate, because PY Rosinante can drag the game for a very long time.

Just keep in mind that if they are starving you, your resource management becomes extremely important. This is why cards like Senior Pink can really help you in this matchup as well.

Just like against BY Teach, your main focus should be removing their key threats from the board, while at the same time trying to ask for resources from them. You also need to take into account that they have access to cards like I Love You, which can take the threats we just removed from their trash back to their hand and allow them to replay those cards.

Usually, our main objective should be to deny their value generation while also putting enough pressure on them, so they do not get the chance to have comfortable value turns. For example, against 6c Rosilaw, being low on DON!! is usually one of the best things you can do.

Having access to multiple Senior Pink is also very good, because it allows us to remove their 8k bodies back to back while continuing to push with our pre-existing board.

At the same time, developing threats like Reiju is very important, because she is really annoying for them to remove. She is also one of the cards we can use to pressure them, together with our 6c Enel and 1-cost characters.

### General tips

- PY Rosinante is one of the trickiest matchups because proper DON!! management is crucial, especially in the early game, where you can deny their Law effects and slow down their entire curve.
- Going second is preferred, because you can pass on specific DON!! numbers, like 1 DON!! and 3 DON!!, to prevent cards like 4c Law and 6c Rosilaw from ramping or drawing.
- Be careful with when you swing with 6c Enel, because PY Rosinante is one of the few decks that can actually remove it, especially through plays involving 7c Sakazuki and Mamaragan.
- Your main removal targets are 7c Sakazuki and 6c Rosilaw. 7c Sakazuki should usually be removed first because it gives them back a lot of DON!!, while 6c Rosilaw requires a double hit because it can be protected once by the Leader effect.
- Commit to Gamma Knife turns only when you are sure you can actually remove the target. Against 6c Rosilaw, one removal effect is often not enough, so you need to plan combinations like a swing plus El Thor, or multiple attacks.
- In the late game, keep denying their value generation while pressuring them with your pre-existing board. Cards like Senior Pink, Reiju, 6c Enel, and your 1-cost characters are very important to remove their 8k bodies and keep pushing before they can stabilize with cards like I Love You.

## 3.4 B Yamato (noir)

**Difficulty: Easy — Preferred Start: First**

### Overview

This is one of the newest decks in the format, and to be honest, I expected it to be much more popular in the meta. However, the more tournaments I play, the more I realize that this deck is not that popular in the end. I will still include it in the guide because I think Black Yamato is not a bad deck in the meta after all, but I do not think we will see it too much.

This is one of those matchups where, if you decide to play Holly, it can make the matchup much better, since it helps you apply the pressure you need against them. However, overall, I still think this is an easy matchup no matter which build you decide to play.

In general, Yamato is a deck that relies a lot on big 8k swings. They usually have two main options: developing the 8c Yamato with the On K.O. effect that brings back a 5k Yamato, or developing the other 8c Yamato, which gets back one card from trash and can play a small character.

Usually, our general game plan is to **counter the small swings and take the big ones**. It is really important to understand this, especially early on. If they go for small swings, we want to counter those, while still developing our usual game plan: going wide with 1-cost characters, 4-cost bodies, and 6c Enel, while removing whatever threats we actually need to remove from the board.

If they decide to play the 8c Yamato that can be destroyed, so the one without the On K.O. effect, we usually want to remove it with Gamma Knife most of the time. If they play the other one, I would rather ignore it and go face.

Remember that Yamato has a lot of 2k counters, so the swings we usually want to make are either 5k, 7k, or higher. I would avoid going for 6k swings unless you need the extra DON!! at the end of the turn to develop another character.

They also have a lot of 1-cost characters that they can play to search. I would usually tend to ignore those, but if you expose your own 1-cost characters, just remember that you always need to establish a new one so you can swing again on the following turn.

Be sure to also remove their early pieces, such as 6k Kin'emon. Then, once they start playing their 8k bodies, you will need to speed up a little bit and put a lot of pressure on them.

**Mulligan cheatsheet** (`media/mulligan-b-yamato.png`) : Ohm, Shura, Charlotte Pudding, Satori, **6c Enel**.

### Early game

For the early game, the most important thing is to get rid of their first threats. Black Yamato usually wants to develop 6k Kin'emon early on, so that is one of the cards we usually want to remove.

From our side, developing cards like Reiju early is very good, because she can deny some swings on the turn we play her while also gaining us cards. Of course, developing an early 6c Enel is also very strong, because if we play it early, it will give us a lot of pressure without needing too much additional investment.

One of the most dangerous things against Black Yamato is not developing a meaningful board. For example, just going wide with 1-cost characters is not going to be enough, because we will not have enough pressure on them. Once they start playing the big Yamato bodies, the pressure they put on us will become much higher.

For this reason, the early game should be focused on removing any small threat they play. For example, if they play 6c Yamato on their 6 DON!! turn and they do not have an 8k Yamato in trash, which almost never happens, we obviously need to remove it.

From our side, we really want to play multiple 4-cost characters together with 6c Enel. The early game should be focused on removing the small threats we already mentioned, while also putting pressure on their Leader.

When attacking, prioritize 5k and 7k swings, because we would rather make different swings that can actually inflict damage instead of giving them efficient counter value. Their deck has access to a lot of 2k counters, so we do not want them to get better value when we start attacking. For example, doing two 6k swings is usually worse than doing one 5k and one 7k swing.

### Mid game

The most important thing is that we always keep pressure on our opponent, and whenever we see a Yamato that we can actually clear, we just remove it.

Of course, having multiple Gamma Knife and Senior Pink can help a lot, especially in the games where they do not see the other Yamato, the one that is basically not worth removing.

I would not focus on using Gamma Knife on the 8k Yamato and then investing Mamaragan or multiple swings into it, because we would lose too much time and too many attacks. I would rather just remove the other 8c Yamato, the one that can actually be destroyed without giving them too much value.

Having said that, depending on how many big Yamato they see, we need to keep up with the pressure and advance our game plan while also dealing with the pressure they are putting on us.

For this reason, it is really important in this phase to have multiple 4-cost bodies that can help us apply enough pressure while also blocking if needed. For example, having a Franky that can block an 8k swing can be very good, or having double 6c Enel that we can use for the rest of the game to pressure them while dealing with the board can be one of the best situations.

Usually, in this part of the game, we do not really need to focus on removing anything that is not an 8k Yamato. If they reach the point where they play something like 6k Blocker Momonosuke, then of course we can remove it with combinations like Lightning Beast Kiten plus Mamaragan, Gamma Knife plus El Thor, or Ohm on board plus Mamaragan.

If you have options in this phase to give double -1 cost to the 8k Yamato with the On K.O. effect, you can also consider doing that and then freezing it with Lightning Dragon. This will basically gain you an extra life, because that Yamato will not be able to swing on the following turn. So keep in mind that having Lightning Dragon together with -1 cost effects can be very good to gain a lot of time in this phase.

### Late game

For the last part of the game, the most important thing to remember is that Black Yamato is a very aggressive deck, and they can perform several swings in a single turn.

What I would recommend for this phase is to learn which combos Yamato can perform with 5c Momonosuke and 2c Shinobu, because those cards allow them to create multiple attacks in one turn.

For this reason, it is really important to stay at least at 2 life in this phase, because usually you want to tank the 8k swings and counter the smaller ones. Remember that in the late game, they might present a turn with multiple swings like 7k, 7k, 7k, and 8k, so knowing that they can create a lot of attacks is very important.

You should also keep in mind that the last swing can be unblockable, so relying too much on Blockers is not always ideal. In this matchup, relying on your hand size is usually better.

Try to visualize how much time you have left in this part of the game and start setting up your lethal. Of course, the more Enel you have on board, the better.

If you swing with Reiju, they can trade into it, so just remember that if you expose her, it will be easier for them to remove her with an 8k swing.

In general, try to play for the damage race. Most of the time, both players will keep going face, and the only cards they will realistically trade into are cards like Franky or Reiju. They will most likely ignore your Enel, so manage your life properly, keep gaining time, and understand the clock you are playing under.

### General tips

- Black Yamato is generally an easy matchup, even though it can become dangerous if you do not develop a meaningful board early enough.
- In the early game, focus on removing their first threats, especially 6k Kin'emon, while developing Reiju, 4-cost bodies, and 6c Enel to start applying pressure.
- Against their big Yamato bodies, you usually want to remove the 8c Yamato that can be destroyed with Gamma Knife, while ignoring the one with the On K.O. effect most of the time and pushing face instead.
- Be careful with your swing numbers, because Black Yamato has access to a lot of 2k counters. Prioritize 5k and 7k swings instead of giving them efficient counter value with repeated 6k swings.
- In the mid game, keep pressure while developing multiple meaningful bodies like Franky, Reiju, and 6c Enel. Cards like Lightning Dragon plus -1 cost effects can also buy you time against their big Yamato swings.
- In the late game, remember that Black Yamato can perform multiple attacks in one turn with cards like 5c Momonosuke and 2c Shinobu. Stay at least at 2 life, tank the big swings, counter the smaller ones, and set up your lethal before they close the game.
